# Vermont Larner College of Medicine - Professionalism Draft

I believe what we colloquially refer to as professionalism is a manifestation of deeper, core values. When referring to professionalism, we may think of things like arriving on time, dressing appropriately, communicating respectfully, and completing one’s responsibilities. Yet I posit that behaviors like the aforementioned serve as a facade for more.

I have come to see professionalism as the unrelenting will to stick to one’s virtues, even when it is inconvenient, humiliating, or disagreeable. In essence, the willingness to put another person’s needs above one’s own serves as the framework for professionalism. As I reflect on what professionalism means to me, I am reminded of an incident involving Beverley, my hospice client.

At ninety-four, Beverley understandably disliked the physical therapy exercises her daughter hoped she would complete. Before exercising, she would often coop herself up in the restroom, refusing to come out. At first, I tried persistent knocking and questioning, but this only deepened her reluctance and left her daughter disappointed. By degrees, I realized that persistence alone was ineffective. Rather than press her, I waited patiently until she emerged on her own terms, then completed the exercises alongside her.

From this experience, I learned that professionalism is not forceful compliance, but the willingness, despite its inconvenience, to meet another person halfway. I have therefore come to consider patience a major part of my core values—one I hope to carry into a career in medicine as I continue developing as a professional.
