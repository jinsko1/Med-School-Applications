# Vermont Larner College of Medicine - Why Larner Draft

As I expounded upon in a previous essay, what makes Larner especially attractive to me is, in part, its integrated curriculum: the VIC. I lean toward the liberal sides of the SPICES model of medical education, and the VIC aligns with my views regarding active participation in one’s own education. A curriculum that intertwines multiple disciplines into one coherent education is meaningful to me because it remains attached to the communities medicine is ultimately meant to serve.

As such, Larner’s Doctoring in Vermont course is especially appealing. Practicing examination skills alongside a preceptor would allow me to mature as a professional in a pragmatic setting where active learning matters most. Likewise, the Public Health Projects would allow me to extend my interest in service beyond individual encounters by applying epidemiological principles to local communities.

Additionally, I am interested in Larner’s commitment to rural and underserved health through the Office of Primary Care and Area Health Education Centers Program. As someone from California, I am drawn to working in a state where different social circumstances and communities shape access to care in ways distinct from my own background. As I discuss in my personal statement, I hope to work with underserved communities as a healthcare professional.

I hope to bring to Larner a willingness to learn actively and serve patiently. In return, I believe Larner would train me to become a physician who embodies the virtues of scientific veracity and community practice.
