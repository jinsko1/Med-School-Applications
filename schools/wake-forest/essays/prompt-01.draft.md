# Wake Forest University School of Medicine - Improve Health for All Draft

Quality medical care ought to be gestalt in nature: each facet of care must be consciously and constantly furthered to improve patient wellbeing, resulting in a practice so integrated and whole that it manifests as equitable care, improving health for all.

This requires deliberately broadening one’s perspective, particularly through meaningful conversation with individuals from diverse populations. From my experience, such conversation reveals how we can serve different needs through advocacy.

My time in student government educated me greatly in this respect. While staffing a feedback tent to gather student suggestions for improving university facilities, I spoke with a student who called attention to the humiliating lack of menstrual products in restrooms. After speaking with two peers, we researched the issue further and brought it to the next meeting for discussion. After additional meetings and careful budgeting, we installed sanitary pads in the university union and recreation center restrooms.

Experiences such as these have taught me the importance of furthering one’s perspective: in this case, identifying a shameful lack of menstrual products. Only through receptiveness in conversation can one truly be inclusive and improve health for all.
