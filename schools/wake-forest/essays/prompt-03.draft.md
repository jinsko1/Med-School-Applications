# Wake Forest University School of Medicine - Most Formative Experience Draft

He had sketched, across the thin pages of his notebook, a rough design for the tattoo he hoped to get once he was released. When I asked about its different aspects—part of which was derived from the ones already on his arm—he confided that the text, written in barbed font, was his daughter’s name.

He is seventeen; his daughter is three.

Among the youth I encountered in juvenile hall, I was most struck by the lived difference between myself and A. He had endured what no child deserves to endure, and had been convicted of attempted murder—the charge that brought him there. And yet, in casual conversation, one would never discern such a grave history.

A ferocious player of Egyptian War—as I learned through repeated losses—A spent his solitude writing lyrics, which he would show me when I volunteered. By degrees, through card games, conversations, and his art, I came to know him beyond his charge.

Volunteering in juvenile hall taught me the value of meeting people across vastly different circumstances. It remains the most formative experience leading me toward medicine—a discipline which I believe is grounded in listening.
