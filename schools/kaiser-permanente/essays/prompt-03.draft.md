# Kaiser Permanente Bernard J. Tyson School of Medicine - Lifelong Learning Draft

I touched upon this briefly within my primary application, but I will further elaborate here on my love for reading—fiction and nonfiction—and how this serves as an intellectual praxis, therefore making me a better future physician.

When reading fiction, one participates in an exchange of consciousnesses, as it were, temporarily occupying another mind. In this state, the reader is introduced to new experiences, social circumstances, and perspectives hitherto unknown. I believe this makes me more receptive to new ideas, more understanding of others, and more culturally aware.

The merits of nonfiction are quite obvious, so I will attempt to explain the more overlooked aspects of it. Students learn quite a bit from attending class, but this alone amounts to a knowledge base that is, broadly speaking, homogeneous. Everyone is learning the same thing; the knowledge base is not diverse. Reading outside of class allows me to pursue questions that are not assigned.

Further, as a physician, reading and learning on one’s own is the expectation. As the literature changes and standards of care shift, one must learn to be critical and keep up with one’s field. Accordingly, I frequently read clinical trials and blog posts from various evidence-based thinkers, though I still do not understand a substantial portion of what I read. Being able to derive enjoyment from this process is a lifelong gift, a never-ending intellectual endeavor. Through reading, I hope to preserve the habit of reflection that lifelong learning requires.
