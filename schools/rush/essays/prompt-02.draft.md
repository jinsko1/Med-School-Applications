# Rush Medical College at Rush University - Distance Traveled - Adversities Draft

Through my experience working in hospice care, I have come to understand that quality care extends beyond knowing what would benefit them.

Understandably, at ninety-four years old, my hospice client did not enjoy the physical therapy exercises her daughter expected her to complete. Before exercising, she would often coop herself up in the restroom, refusing to come out. Persistent knocking made little progress, leaving me unsure how to respond and her daughter disappointed.

At first, I understood my responsibility too precisely. Because the exercises were meant to preserve her strength, I thought perseverance meant continuing to press her. Gradually, I came to see the situation more carefully. Her daughter wanted her mother to remain as healthy as possible; my client’s refusal expressed fatigue, discomfort, and perhaps a wish to retain agency over a body increasingly managed by others. I learnt that medicine requires attention to dignity and deeper messages behind refusal, especially when persistence becomes an imposition.
