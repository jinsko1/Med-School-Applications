# Rush Medical College at Rush University - Privilege and Health Equity Draft

A question that demanded sustained inquiry from me arose in my microbiology research on urinary tract infections. A strain I was studying failed to exhibit swarming motility. While other strains spread across the agar plate, this mutant remained at the point of inoculation, repeatedly.

Because the problem was obstinate, I spoke with my PI and lab members to approach it differently. We discussed possible explanations, returned repeatedly to the literature, adjusted experiments, and ran growth curves and prepared agar plates all summer. Though tedious, each attempt clarified a small portion of the problem, even when it did not provide a conclusive answer.

This changed how I understood discovery. I had imagined research as a more direct movement from question to answer, but the project taught me that knowledge often advances by degrees. As a future physician, I hope to preserve this willingness to remain curious even when certainty is unavailable.

During my service in student government, I coauthored a proposal to install sanitary pads in restrooms of the university union and recreation center, fostering a more inclusive campus for the student body. The problem was identified through student interaction: basic health needs were not being met, partly due to inaccessibility and STI stigma.

The project required persistence because a seemingly simple proposal became, in practice, a logistical and bureaucratic problem. Working with peers and campus partners, we considered where students were most likely to need these products, which restrooms would be most accessible, and how the proposal could be implemented.

What kept me engaged was the knowledge that the issue was mundane precisely because it was important. After much planning, we passed the proposal, and pads were installed. This taught me that leadership often requires translating concern into practice while remaining with unglamorous steps so that they become a reality.
