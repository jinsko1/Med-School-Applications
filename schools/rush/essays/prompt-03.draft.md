# Rush Medical College at Rush University - Distance Traveled - Effect and Growth Draft

Many of the youth I encountered in juvenile hall had lived without a sense of stability. Oftentimes, the closest thing to constancy came from friends and from the people who continued to show up for them. As such, I committed myself to volunteering consistently, knowing that trust could not be built through occasional presence.

What drew me initially was the opportunity to serve youth whose lives had been shaped by circumstances beyond their control. What kept me engaged was the realization that presence itself could become meaningful. Over time, I learned that commitment required active engagement. I would actively ask questions, making the effort to know each youth as an individual.

When they invited me to play basketball, asked me to join card games, or showed me their artwork, I understood these moments as small signs of trust. Service is not an abstract virtue, but a repeated decision to remain long enough for another person to believe one’s presence is sincere.
