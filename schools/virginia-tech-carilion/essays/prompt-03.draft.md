# Virginia Tech Carilion School of Medicine - Greatest Generational Challenge Draft

I chiefly consider the ability to form lasting relationships at risk of being compromised. With individuals becoming ever more distant—politically, socially, and digitally—I often find myself worried for my generation. Perhaps because of this distance, we may find others easy to presume upon with no information, categorizing them into modest bins and retreating from people simply because their experiences do not align with ours.

As I expounded upon in a previous essay, my service in juvenile hall taught me how easily a person can be reduced to the most visible or troubling part of their history. Hospice volunteering has also taught me the same lesson in a slightly different form: trust cannot be demanded, but gained through patience. Both experiences share the same fundamental kernel of human relationships: one cannot presume, and must persist in understanding with patience, even when experiences and perspectives may be vastly different.

I may not be able to address this issue at scale, but I can address it on an individual basis through medicine. I find a career in medicine well suited for this role, as physicians must listen carefully before hastily making presumptuous conclusions, treating each patient as a fellow human whose dignity cannot be reduced to circumstance or mere impression.
