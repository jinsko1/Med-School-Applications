# University of Illinois College of Medicine - Perspective and Contribution to UICOM Draft

One aspect of my perspective that is especially meaningful to me is my belief that no person should be reduced to the most visible or troubling part of their history.

In juvenile hall, I saw how easily this reduction can occur. I was especially struck by “A.” He had endured what no child should endure, yet in ordinary conversation, one would never discern such a grave history. I am ashamed to admit that I first assumed a distance between us so great that I could hardly comprehend it. By degrees, through card games, conversations, and his art, I came to know him beyond his charge. This challenged how I understood circumstance and character. Had I been born into a childhood as adverse as his, even if fundamentally myself, my life could have followed a vastly different path.

This experience taught me to ask questions before forming judgments. In a collaborative learning environment, I hope this would make me a peer who listens carefully and resists biased presumptions, remaining receptive to perspectives hitherto unknown.

Further, my love for reading—fiction and nonfiction—serves as an intellectual praxis. When reading fiction, one participates in an exchange of consciousnesses, temporarily occupying another mind. Together, service and reading have made me more receptive, more understanding of others, and more prepared to contribute to an enriching learning environment.
