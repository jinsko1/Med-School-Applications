# University of Massachusetts Chan Medical School - Project, Solution, and Effectiveness Draft

During my service in student government, I coauthored a proposal to install sanitary pads in restrooms of the university union and recreation center, fostering a more inclusive campus for the student body. The problem was identified through student interaction: unfortunately, basic health needs were not being met, partly due to inaccessibility, partly due to STI stigma.

To combat this, we first took into consideration where students were most likely to need these products, preferably in private spaces, which restrooms would be most accessible, and how, logistically, the proposal could be implemented through collaboration with campus partners. After much budgeting and bureaucratic work, we passed the proposal, and sanitary pads were installed in the university union and recreation center. My term also saw the installation of three healthcare product vending machines across campus, making products from birth control to analgesics available to students.

The effectiveness of these efforts was measured through how often the products were replenished. In essence, both accomplishments resulted from identifying issues through student interaction and taking initiative to act on them through collaboration and inclusive practice, while keeping practical implementation in mind.
