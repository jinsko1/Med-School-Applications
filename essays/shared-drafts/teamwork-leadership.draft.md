# Teamwork / Leadership Shared Draft

Collaboration often begins with the acknowledgment that one person alone cannot meet every demand. As an individual, I can at times become ossified in my own way of thinking; another person’s insight can lift me out of that fixation and help me approach a problem anew.

I encountered this often as a learning assistant in the chemistry department, where I helped students work through class material. The ways in which I understand a problem are not always the ways in which others learn it. At times, despite repeated explanations, a student would still struggle to grasp the concept before them.

This became especially clear while students were learning thermodynamics, a subject many found difficult. When my own explanations failed to help, I turned to the other learning assistants working alongside me. Their perspectives were invaluable. They offered different analogies, methods, and ways of framing the same problem, often reaching students in ways I could not.

Collaboration does not merely entail dividing work among people. It is the humility to recognize the limits of one’s own perspective and to rely on others, so that together, we can better serve the needs of others.
