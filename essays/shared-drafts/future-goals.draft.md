# Future Goals Shared Draft

Over the next decade, I hope to see the field evolve into a more inclusive form of evidence-based medicine, one that advocates for addressing the lack of research in certain populations, thus questioning whom interventions work for, addressing, in particular, the disconcerting lack of research in women’s health.

My research in UTIs revealed this disparity, as I found the literature concerning UTIs in women shamefully sparse. On top of the myriad of health problems that post-menopausal women face, recurrent urinary tract infections cause significant discomfort and economic burden yet remain understudied, revealing a desperate need for trials that evaluate the treatment and prophylaxis of UTIs in women.

The biased evidence base is especially true of clinical trials, and to imagine that many providers base their care on skewed populations appalls me. I find rectifying these gaps in medicine a priority in the coming years and therefore hope to see a more inclusive form of healthcare that serves patients equitably.
